Slide 1: Introduction to Request for Payment (RFP)
Title: Request for Payment (RFP) Using TCH RTP and FedNow
Content:
	•	Definition of RFP: Request for Payment (RFP), also known as RfP or Request for Pay, is a messaging framework in instant payment systems where a payee (the party receiving funds) initiates a non-value message to request a specific payment from a payer (the party sending funds). The payer reviews the details—such as amount, due date, and invoice information—and can approve, decline, or respond, triggering an instant credit transfer if approved. This puts the payer in control and uses a push payment model, ensuring funds are transferred in real-time without debits. 16 18 20 29 
	•	RFP is supported on both The Clearing House (TCH) Real-Time Payments (RTP) network and the Federal Reserve’s FedNow Service, enabling 24/7/365 instant settlements between participating banks. 26 22 
	•	Key Benefits: Faster collections, reduced fraud risk, and improved cash flow for businesses and individuals.

Slide 2: Overview of TCH RTP and FedNow
Title: Understanding the Platforms: TCH RTP and FedNow
Content:
	•	TCH RTP (Real-Time Payments): Operated by The Clearing House (TCH), RTP is a private-sector instant payment rail launched in 2017. It supports credit transfers up to $1 million per transaction, with irrevocable settlements in seconds. RFP functionality allows payees to send requests directly to payers’ banks for approval and instant payment. 25 32 40 
	•	FedNow Service: Launched by the Federal Reserve in 2023, FedNow is a public instant payment system with a current limit of $500,000 per transaction (potentially increasing). It also uses ISO 20022 messaging for RFPs, enabling requests for payments that payers can fulfill instantly via their financial institutions. 27 39 14 
	•	Similarities: Both are always-on, use push payments, and integrate RFP for requesting funds across banks without needing account details upfront.
	•	Differences: RTP has broader adoption among large banks; FedNow focuses on inclusivity for smaller institutions.

Slide 3: Key Use Cases for RFP
Title: Use Cases for Request for Payment
Content:
	•	RFP enables a wide range of scenarios where instant, secure payment requests are needed, particularly in B2B, B2C, and P2P contexts. 2 3 4 13 
	•	Risky or Pre-Collection Payments: Billers request payment before services end (e.g., utilities before disconnection) to mitigate non-payment risks.
	•	Ad Hoc Payments: For one-off transactions like freelance work, repairs, or event fees, where a quick request speeds up settlement.
	•	E-commerce and Recurring Payments: Merchants send RFPs for online purchases or subscriptions, allowing customers to approve instantly via their bank app.
	•	B2B Invoicing: Businesses request payments for invoices, enabling faster cash flow without traditional wire transfers.
	•	Instant Payroll or Gig Economy: Employers or platforms request worker approval for immediate disbursements, or workers request advances.
	•	Additional uses include insurance claims, real estate earnest money, and emergency fund transfers.

Slide 4: Highlighting Transfers from External Banks
Title: RFP for Transfers from External Bank to Your Account
Content:
	•	RFP excels in facilitating transfers from an external bank account (payer’s) to your account (payee’s), even across different financial institutions, via interconnected RTP or FedNow networks. 38 33 41 
	•	Process Overview:
	1	Payee (you) sends an RFP message via your bank to the payer’s bank, including amount, description, and due date.
	2	Payer receives the request (e.g., via mobile banking app or email/SMS notification) and reviews details.
	3	Payer approves, initiating an instant credit push from their external account to yours.
	4	Funds settle in seconds, with confirmation to both parties; irrevocable once sent.
	•	Key Highlight: This cross-bank capability reduces friction in collections, as no shared account details are needed—ideal for external vendors or customers.
	•	Limitations: Payer must have an RFP-enabled account; not all banks participate yet.

Slide 5: Conclusion and Benefits
Title: Why Adopt RFP with TCH RTP and FedNow?
Content:
	•	Summary: RFP transforms payment requests into seamless, real-time transactions across TCH RTP and FedNow, with strong use cases in billing, e-commerce, and cross-bank transfers.
	•	Benefits:
	◦	Instant settlements improve liquidity and reduce days sales outstanding (DSO).
	◦	Enhanced security: Payer-controlled pushes minimize fraud compared to pulls like ACH debits.
	◦	Convenience: Digital requests via apps eliminate paper invoices.
	◦	Scalability: Supports growing adoption, with over 500 institutions on RTP and expanding FedNow participation. 24 10 
	•	Next Steps: Integrate RFP via your bank’s API or payment processor for faster receivables.
